# Example — Post Hook Generator

Illustrative example of what such a prompt **can** produce. The response was generated by an AI in one pass — **not guaranteed, and not professional advice**. All names, numbers, and details are fictional.

## Prompt (filled)

> You are a direct-response copywriter specializing in first lines. Write me opening hooks that create a gap the reader must close by reading on.
>
> MY DETAILS
> - The post topic and its core point: freelancers underprice because they quote hourly — value pricing earns more and filters bad clients
> - Platform: LinkedIn
> - Audience: Arab freelancers
> - Tone: contrarian
> - Language: English with Arabic option
>
> YOUR TASK — answer in this exact order:
> 1. TEN HOOKS: 10 first lines in my tone — at least 2 curiosity hooks, 2 number/stat hooks, 2 story-opening hooks, 2 contrarian hooks, 2 pain-point hooks.
> 2. TOP 3 RANKED: which 3 fit my topic best and one line each on why they'll work for my audience.
> 3. FIRST PARAGRAPH: for my #1 hook, the 2 sentences that follow it and keep the promise.
> 4. ANTI-CLICHÉ CHECK: flag any hook I should avoid because it's overused on the platform.
>
> RULES
> - If critical information is missing, ask up to 3 clarifying questions first; otherwise proceed with clearly-stated assumptions.
> - Hooks are max 12 words and never clickbait — the post must deliver what the hook promises.
> - No "🚀 Excited to announce..." energy unless I ask for it.
>
> OUTPUT FORMAT
> - Ten hooks first, labeled by type; then the top-3 ranking; then the first paragraph; then the anti-cliché check.

## Example AI response

**1. TEN HOOKS**

1. (contrarian) Hourly billing is why your best clients pay you the least.
2. (contrarian) The freelancer who charges per hour is subsidizing their slowest client.
3. (stat) I raised my rates 3x and lost only 1 of 12 clients.
4. (stat) 40 projects in: hourly invoicing cost me roughly 60,000 EGP I'll never recover.
5. (story) The day I stopped selling hours, a client tripled my budget.
6. (story) My first client paid me 300 EGP for 30 hours of work. My fault, not his.
7. (curiosity) There's a line in your proposal that's quietly cutting your price.
8. (curiosity) The cheapest freelancers on this platform have one thing in common.
9. (pain) You finished the project in half the estimated hours. You still got paid for the estimate — if you price on value.
10. (pain) Every "quick call" you don't charge for is a discount you didn't choose.

**2. TOP 3 RANKED**
- #3 — a concrete rate story with a survivable loss number reads as experience, not bragging; Arab freelancer audiences respond to real numbers.
- #1 — names the enemy (hourly billing) in one line; contrarian openers earn comments from people who disagree.
- #9 — the unchosen discount is a pain every freelancer has felt this month.

**3. FIRST PARAGRAPH (for #3)**
> I didn't get braver. I got better at explaining outcomes. The one client who left was the one who wanted an employee, not a partner — and that was the lesson.

**4. ANTI-CLICHÉ CHECK**
Skip any version of "Your rates are your fault" — overused on LinkedIn in 2025–2026; #1 says the same thing with a specific mechanism.
