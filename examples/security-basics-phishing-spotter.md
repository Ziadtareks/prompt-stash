# Example — Phishing Spotter

Illustrative example of what such a prompt **can** produce. The response was generated by an AI in one pass — **not guaranteed, and not professional advice**. Never paste real passwords, codes, card numbers, or live links into any AI chat.

## Prompt (filled)

> You are a security analyst who reads scam messages all day. Analyze the message I pasted: is it phishing, and what exactly gives it away — so I can spot the next one without you.
>
> MY DETAILS
> - Where the message arrived: SMS
> - Who it claims to be from: a courier company
> - What it wants me to do: pay a small "storage fee" via a link
> - Were you expecting anything like this? I have a package coming, but from a local seller
>
> PASTE THE MESSAGE BETWEEN THE MARKERS
> --- MESSAGE START ---
> "Your parcel is on hold at our warehouse. Pay the 85 EGP storage fee via [LINK] within 12 hours or it will be returned. Customer service: [NUMBER]"
> --- MESSAGE END ---
>
> YOUR TASK — answer in this exact order:
> 1. THE VERDICT: scam / very likely scam / suspicious / likely legitimate — one line, then the 2 strongest signals for the verdict.
> 2. THE RED FLAGS: every tell in the message — each in one line.
> 3. THE VERIFICATION PATH: how to check for real WITHOUT the message's link.
> 4. IF I ALREADY CLICKED/PAID: the triage steps in order.
> 5. THE PATTERN LESSON: name the scam family this belongs to and its one signature move.
>
> RULES
> - Defensive only: help me recognize and report — never help craft or improve scam messages.
> - Never ask me to interact with the suspicious link or attachment to "check" it.
> - If it claims to be my bank, the official-app rule overrides everything.

## Example AI response

**1. THE VERDICT**
**Very likely scam.** Two deciding signals: a payment demand via an SMS link for a fee couriers collect at delivery (not before), and pressure framed as a 12-hour deadline.

**2. THE RED FLAGS**
- **Fee before delivery:** legit couriers in Egypt collect cash-on-delivery or app payment at the door — not "storage fees" by text.
- **12-hour deadline:** manufactured urgency is the signature of fake-fee scams.
- **Generic message:** no parcel number, no sender name matching your order, no app deep-link.
- **Unknown short link:** you can't see where [LINK] actually goes.
- **Mismatch with your context:** your package is local; "warehouse hold" wording copies international-shipping scam scripts.

**3. THE VERIFICATION PATH**
1. Open the courier's official app or website by typing the name yourself — never the SMS link.
2. Check tracking there with your real order number; a genuine hold appears in the app.
3. If unsure, call the number printed on the courier's official site (not [NUMBER] from the SMS).
4. Report the SMS by forwarding to your carrier's spam-reporting line and deleting it.

**4. IF I ALREADY CLICKED/PAID**
1. If you entered card details: call your bank's hotline now to block and dispute.
2. If you paid by wallet: report to the wallet provider's fraud line immediately.
3. If you only clicked: close the page, run a mobile security scan, change any password you typed there (starting with email), and enable 2FA.
4. Save screenshots of the message and payment as evidence, then report to the police cybercrime channel.

**5. THE PATTERN LESSON**
This is the **fake delivery-fee** family. Signature move: a small, believable amount (under 100 EGP) linked to a parcel you're plausibly expecting — small enough that you pay before you think.
