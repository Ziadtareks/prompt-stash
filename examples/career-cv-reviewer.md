# Example — CV Reviewer

Demonstration of [`prompts/career/cv-reviewer.txt`](../prompts/career/cv-reviewer.txt) with the variables filled in. Illustrative example of what such a prompt **can** produce. The response was generated by an AI in one pass — **not guaranteed, and not professional advice**. All names, numbers, and details are fictional (Egypt-plausible where relevant).

## Prompt (filled)

> You are a technical recruiter who screens 100 CVs a day. Review my CV against the specific job and tell me what a 6-second skim would miss.
>
> MY DETAILS
> - Target job title and key requirements from the posting: Data analyst: SQL, Power BI, stakeholder reporting, 3+ years experience
> - My industry and experience level: finance graduate, 2 years reporting
> - What results I'm getting (applications sent vs interviews): 40 applications, 1 interview
>
> PASTE YOUR CV BETWEEN THE MARKERS
> --- CV START ---
> Omar Khaled — hardworking finance professional seeking growth opportunities.
Experience: Al-Nour Trading Co., Accountant (2024–present): responsible for accounting tasks, monthly reports, and helping with audits. Eshta Trading, Junior Accountant (2023–2024): data entry, invoices, assisting senior accountants.
Skills: Excel, Power BI, communication, teamwork.
Education: BCom Accounting, Cairo University.
> --- CV END ---
>
> YOUR TASK — answer in this exact order:
> 1. THE 6-SECOND TEST: what a recruiter sees in the first screen of my CV — and whether the target job's keywords appear in it.
> 2. KEYWORD GAPS: requirements from Data analyst: SQL, Power BI, stakeholder reporting, 3+ years experience that my CV demonstrably matches but never mentions by name (the ATS and the skimmer both need the words).
> 3. FINDINGS TABLE: Section | Problem | Fix — covering summary, experience bullets (weak verbs, no numbers), formatting risks (tables/graphics that break parsers), and length.
> 4. THE REWRITE SAMPLE: my 3 weakest bullets rewritten with action + method + measurable result (using my real facts; [X%] placeholders where I must add numbers).
> 5. THE VERDICT: send it, fix-and-send, or rebuild — one line, based on 40 applications, 1 interview.
>
> RULES
> - Input-complete prompt: if the pasted material is unreadable or clearly incomplete, say exactly what is missing instead of proceeding on assumptions.
> - Judge against Data analyst: SQL, Power BI, stakeholder reporting, 3+ years experience, not against a perfect imaginary CV.
> - Never invent experience, tools, or numbers — placeholders where I must supply facts.
> - Formatting advice must survive real ATS parsing, not just look pretty.
>
> OUTPUT FORMAT
> - Five numbered sections: 6-second test, keyword gaps, findings table (Section | Problem | Fix), 3 bullet rewrites with [FILL] marks, verdict (send / fix-and-send / rebuild). Lead with THE 6-SECOND TEST.
>

## Example AI response

**1. THE 6-SECOND TEST**
A recruiter sees: "hardworking finance professional seeking growth opportunities," two accountant titles, and a skills line. The words **SQL**, **Power BI**, **reporting to stakeholders**, and **analyst** — the exact terms this posting screens for — never appear near the top. Power BI exists but buried in a 4-item skills list with no evidence. In 6 seconds this reads as "accountant," not "data analyst."

**2. KEYWORD GAPS**
Your experience likely matches these requirements, but the CV never says the words:
- **SQL** — if you've queried the accounting system or built extracts, say so; if not, this is your biggest gap to close before applying further.
- **Stakeholder reporting** — "monthly reports" hides this. Did you present to managers? Who acted on your reports?
- **Data analysis** — "helping with audits" and "data entry" undersell reconciliation and variance analysis work.

**3. FINDINGS TABLE**

| Section | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Summary | Generic objective ("hardworking… seeking growth") — says nothing about analysis | Replace with: "Finance analyst with 2 years of reporting experience — SQL & Power BI — building monthly management reports for [industry]" |
| Experience | Task lists with no outcomes or scale ("responsible for accounting tasks") | Action + method + result: "Automated the monthly close report in Power BI, cutting prep from 3 days to [X] hours" |
| Experience | No numbers anywhere | Add scope: rows processed, reports/month, hours saved — real ones, even modest |
| Skills | Flat 4-item list, no proficiency order | Lead with the job's stack: SQL · Power BI · Excel (advanced) · … |
| Length/format | Fine at this stage | Keep to one page |

**4. THE REWRITE SAMPLE** (my 3 weakest bullets, your facts + [FILL] for real numbers)
- Before: "responsible for monthly reports" → After: "Build [N] monthly management reports in Power BI for [department], covering [KPIs], used by [who] in planning"
- Before: "helping with audits" → After: "Prepared [N] audit schedules covering [X] transactions; cut query time by [Y%] by moving extracts to SQL" *(only if true)*
- Before: "data entry, invoices" → After: "Processed [N] invoices/month with [accuracy metric]; flagged [X] duplicates in the vendor ledger"

**5. THE VERDICT**
**Fix-and-send.** With 40 applications and 1 interview, the market is telling you the CV reads as accountant, not analyst. Fix the summary, pull SQL/Power BI evidence into the bullets, and target roles that accept 2 years; note the posting asks for 3+ — add a line in the cover letter addressing that gap directly.
