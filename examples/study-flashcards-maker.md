# Example — Flashcard Maker

Demonstration of [`prompts/study/flashcards-maker.txt`](../prompts/study/flashcards-maker.txt) with the variables filled in. Illustrative example of what such a prompt **can** produce. The response was generated by an AI in one pass — **not guaranteed, and not professional advice**. All names, numbers, and details are fictional (Egypt-plausible where relevant).

## Prompt (filled)

> You are a flashcard designer who follows the proven rules: one fact per card, question first, answer short. Turn my material into cards I can paste into any flashcard app.
>
> MY DETAILS
> - Number of cards I need: 10
> - Card language (e.g. English, Arabic, or both): English
>
> PASTE YOUR MATERIAL BETWEEN THE MARKERS
> --- MATERIAL START ---
> Photosynthesis: plants convert light energy into chemical energy (glucose) using carbon dioxide and water, releasing oxygen. Happens in the chloroplast, using the pigment chlorophyll. Two stages: light-dependent reactions (in the thylakoid, need light, produce ATP and NADPH, split water) and the Calvin cycle (in the stroma, no light needed, uses ATP + NADPH to turn CO2 into glucose). Limiting factors: light intensity, CO2 concentration, temperature.
> --- MATERIAL END ---
>
> YOUR TASK — answer in this exact order:
> 1. THE CARDS: a table with columns # | Question | Answer. Exactly 10 cards.
> 2. MIX THE TYPES: at least 60% direct questions (What does X mean?), some fill-in-the-blank cards (The mitochondria is the ___ of the cell), and a few compare cards (X vs Y: main difference?).
> 3. HARD ONES FLAGGED: mark the 3 cards I am most likely to get wrong with a ⚠️ and one line on why.
>
> RULES
> - Input-complete prompt: if the pasted material is unreadable or clearly incomplete, say exactly what is missing instead of proceeding on assumptions.
> - One fact per card. If an answer needs "and" twice, split it into two cards.
> - Answers under 15 words where possible.
> - Only use facts from my material — never invent.
> - Write the cards in English.
>
> If the requested output language is Arabic: use Egyptian dialect for casual/social content or Modern Standard Arabic for formal contexts — state which you used — and write natural native phrasing a native reader would actually say, never a stiff translation from English.
>
> OUTPUT FORMAT
> - Three numbered sections: the card table (exactly the requested count; # | Question | Answer), type-mix note with percentages, exactly 3 ⚠️ flags. Lead with THE CARDS.
>

## Example AI response

**1. THE CARDS**

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is photosynthesis? ⚠️ | Plants converting light energy into chemical energy (glucose) from CO2 and water, releasing oxygen |
| 2 | Where in the cell does photosynthesis happen? | The chloroplast |
| 3 | Which pigment absorbs the light? | Chlorophyll |
| 4 | What are the two stages of photosynthesis? | Light-dependent reactions and the Calvin cycle |
| 5 | Where do the light-dependent reactions occur? | In the thylakoid |
| 6 | What do the light-dependent reactions produce? | ATP and NADPH (from splitting water) |
| 7 | The Calvin cycle takes place in the ___ of the chloroplast. | Stroma |
| 8 | The Calvin cycle ___ light directly. | Does not need |
| 9 | Photosynthesis vs. Calvin cycle: what does each need? | Photosynthesis overall needs light; the Calvin cycle stage runs without light but uses ATP + NADPH from the light stage |
| 10 | Name the three limiting factors of photosynthesis. ⚠️ | Light intensity, CO2 concentration, temperature |

**2. MIX THE TYPES**
Direct questions: #1–6 (60%). Fill-in-the-blank: #7–8 (20%). Compare card: #9 (10%) — plus #10 as a recall list, giving 70/20/10 against the 60% floor.

**3. HARD ONES FLAGGED**
- ⚠️ #1: Students define it as "making food" and drop the inputs/outputs (CO2 + water in, glucose + oxygen out) — examiners want the full exchange.
- ⚠️ #9: The classic trap — "Calvin cycle needs no light" gets misread as "the Calvin cycle is what happens in the dark at night." It runs in daylight too; it just doesn't use light directly.
- ⚠️ #10: Three items in one card — normally split, kept together because exam lists ask for all three; memorize as "LCT" (Light, CO2, Temperature).
