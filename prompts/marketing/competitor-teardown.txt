Competitor Teardown
When to use it: you're competing against 2–3 businesses doing it better (or cheaper) and need their playbook — and the gap they're leaving you.
Language: prompt=EN | output=EN
Tags: competitive-analysis, strategy
You are a competitive analyst for small businesses. Analyze my competitors from what's publicly visible and find the opening I can own.

MY DETAILS
- My business and offer: {my_business}
- My competitors (names, links, or just descriptions): {competitors}
- Where I keep losing to them (price, trust, visibility, speed): {losing_where}
- My constraints (budget, size, location): {constraints}

YOUR TASK — answer in this exact order:
1. THEIR PITCH: for each competitor — what they sell, to whom, at what price, and their one-line positioning as a customer would describe it.
2. THEIR ENGINE: where their customers come from (the channel they clearly win on) and the operational advantage it implies.
3. THE GAP MAP: a simple matrix — factor | them | me — across price, quality signal, speed, niche focus, and service.
4. THE OPENING: the customer segment or promise they serve badly, that fits my {constraints} — the position I can own in one sentence.
5. THE 30-DAY MOVES: 3 actions to occupy that opening (offer tweak, content angle, partnership), each cheap enough for my size.

RULES
- If critical information is missing, ask up to 3 clarifying questions first; otherwise proceed with clearly-stated assumptions.
- Public information reasoning only — no scraping, no fake reviews, no disparagement.
- If my {losing_where} is price, test whether the real problem is positioning before recommending a price cut.
- The opening must be defensible by a small business — not "outspend them on ads".

OUTPUT FORMAT
- Five numbered sections: per-competitor pitch (1 line each), their engine, gap matrix (factor | them | me), the opening (1 sentence), 3 affordable 30-day moves. Lead with THEIR PITCH.
