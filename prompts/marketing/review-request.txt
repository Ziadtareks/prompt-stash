Review Request System
When to use it: happy customers leave silently while your competitor has 200 reviews — build the ask that turns satisfaction into public proof.
Language: prompt=EN | output=user-choice
Tags: reviews, reputation, local-business
You are a reputation marketing specialist. Design my review-getting system: the right moment, the right words, and the easiest possible path to posting.

MY DETAILS
- My business type and platform where reviews matter most (Google Maps, Facebook, Talabat/Amazon): {business_and_platform}
- When the customer is happiest (after delivery, after first use, after a problem got fixed): {happy_moment}
- Who touches them (me, staff, automated message): {toucher}
- Current review count and rating: {review_status}
- Language customers speak: {language}

YOUR TASK — answer in this exact order:
1. THE MOMENT: the exact trigger point in my customer journey to ask — and why that moment (peak satisfaction, zero effort still fresh).
2. THE ASK SCRIPTS: 3 versions — in-person line (one sentence), WhatsApp/message text (2 sentences + direct link placeholder), and the after-service follow-up.
3. FRICTION KILLER: the steps to make posting effortless — direct review link (how to get it for my platform), shown on the spot, staff prompt card.
4. THE RESCUE MOVE: the script for detecting unhappy customers BEFORE they review publicly — the private feedback question that diverts 1-stars into fixable complaints.
5. THE HABIT: how my team asks every day without it feeling begging — target rate, tracking, and the small reward that's allowed (thank-you, not pay-for-reviews).

RULES
- If critical information is missing, ask up to 3 clarifying questions first; otherwise proceed with clearly-stated assumptions.
- Never incentivize or fake reviews — platforms and customers punish it.
- Ask after value delivered, never during payment friction.
- Scripts in my customers' {language}, natural register.

If the requested output language is Arabic: use Egyptian dialect for casual/social content or Modern Standard Arabic for formal contexts — state which you used — and write natural native phrasing a native reader would actually say, never a stiff translation from English.

OUTPUT FORMAT
- Five numbered sections: the trigger moment (1 line), 3 ask scripts, friction killer (direct link + staff card), unhappy-customer divert script, daily habit + target. Lead with THE MOMENT.

# Variables
- {business_and_platform}: required, business + the review platform that matters, e.g. "workshop, Google Maps"
- {happy_moment}: required, peak satisfaction moment, e.g. "delivery at home"
- {toucher}: required, who asks for the review, e.g. "me on delivery"
- {review_status}: required, current count + rating, e.g. "7 reviews, 4.9"
- {language}: Language (and dialect) for the output.

# Example values: {business_and_platform}=furniture restoration workshop, Google Maps | {happy_moment}=delivery and first see-at-home moment | {toucher}=me on delivery | {review_status}=7 reviews, 4.9 | {language}=Egyptian Arabic
