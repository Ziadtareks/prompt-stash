Status Update Email Writer
When to use it: your manager asks "any updates?" and you need a status email that reads as "on it" — progress visible, risks flagged early, nothing buried.
Language: prompt=EN | output=user-choice
Tags: work, email, status-update

You are my chief of staff. Turn my messy notes into a status update the reader finishes in 60 seconds and trusts. Progress must be visible, risks stated plainly, and no blocker allowed to hide in the middle of a paragraph.

MY DETAILS
- Who receives this (my manager, client, stakeholder) and how technical they are: {audience}
- Project or workstream name: {project}
- Reporting period: {period}
- My raw notes — progress, blockers, next steps, unpolished: {raw_notes}
- Tone (neutral professional, upbeat, formal): {tone}
- Output language (English, Arabic, or both side by side): {output_language}

YOUR TASK — answer in this exact order:
1. SUBJECT LINE: 2 options under 60 characters — project name plus a status word ("on track", "one blocker") — never just "Update".
2. TL;DR: one sentence — overall state (on track / at risk / blocked) and the single most important fact.
3. PROGRESS: 3-5 bullets of what moved this period, each with a concrete outcome or number from my notes.
4. RISKS & BLOCKERS: bullets for real risks only. Each: what it is, who/what it hits, what I'm already doing. If none: "No blockers this period."
5. NEXT PERIOD: 2-4 bullets of what I'll do next, with dates where my notes allow.
6. FULL EMAIL: everything above assembled — greeting, sections in order, one closing line stating whether I need anything from {audience}.

RULES
- Use only facts from my notes. A missing number, date, or name becomes [FILL: what's needed] — never invented.
- If critical information is missing, ask up to 3 clarifying questions first; otherwise proceed with clearly-stated assumptions.
- No corporate padding ("circling back", "touching base"). Short sentences, active voice.
- If something is late or failing, say it plainly in RISKS & BLOCKERS — do not soften it into disappearance.
- Redact confidential client or financial data before pasting notes; keep generic names in the draft.

If the requested output language is Arabic: use Modern Standard Arabic for a formal client or an Egyptian-dialect workplace tone for an internal manager — state which you used — and write natural native phrasing, never a stiff translation from English.

OUTPUT FORMAT
- Six numbered sections in the order above. Section 6 is the complete email, ≤200 words, TL;DR as its first line. Lead with the SUBJECT LINE options.

# Variables
- {audience}: required, who receives it and their technical level, e.g. "my manager, non-technical"
- {project}: required, project or workstream name, e.g. "website migration to new hosting"
- {period}: required, the reporting window, e.g. "week of Oct 5-9"
- {raw_notes}: required, your unpolished notes on progress, blockers, and next steps
- {tone}: required, e.g. "neutral professional" or "formal"
- {output_language}: required, e.g. "English" or "Arabic (Egyptian)"

# Example values: {audience}=my direct manager, non-technical | {project}=company website migration to new hosting | {period}=week of Oct 5-9 | {raw_notes}=DNS switched Tuesday, old site still up as backup; checkout page retest at 40% success rate, bug ticket filed; waiting on hosting support since Thursday; plan: finish retest next week | {tone}=neutral professional | {output_language}=English
