Late Payment Chaser
When to use it: your invoice is overdue, the friendly reminder failed, and you need an escalation ladder that gets paid without burning the relationship.
Language: prompt=EN | output=EN
Tags: payments, collections, freelance
ℹ️ Planning guidance with stated assumptions — not financial advice.

You are a collections professional with perfect manners. Build my escalation sequence — each message firmer than the last, all of them professional.

MY DETAILS
- Invoice details (number, amount, currency, due date, days overdue): {invoice_details}
- Who owes me (long-term client, new client, big company, friend's business): {client_type}
- Reminders sent so far: {reminders_sent}
- Any excuse they gave (person left, "processing", silent): {excuse}
- My bottom line (stop work, late fee, legal letter): {bottom_line}

YOUR TASK — answer in this exact order:
1. WHERE WE ARE: read {reminders_sent} and {excuse} — which escalation stage applies now.
2. THE NEXT MESSAGE: the email for my current stage — polite, specific (invoice number, amount, days overdue), one clear action with a date.
3. THE FINAL NOTICE: the firm version — consequences named ({bottom_line}), deadline set, zero insults, cc line suggestion if it's a big company (finance department).
4. THE PHONE SCRIPT: 5 lines for calling — opening, the ask, handling "it's processing", agreeing a payment date, confirming in writing.
5. PREVENTION: the 2 contract/hoops changes (deposit %, payment before final files) so this client never does it twice.

RULES
- If critical information is missing, ask up to 3 clarifying questions first; otherwise proceed with clearly-stated assumptions.
- Dates and amounts exactly as I gave them.
- Firmness escalates; rudeness never.
- If {client_type} is a big company, include the "find the accounts payable contact" tactic.
Note: Promise no guaranteed outcomes — state your assumptions explicitly and include one line noting these are planning estimates, not financial advice.
