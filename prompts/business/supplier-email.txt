Supplier Email Writer
When to use it: you need to ask a supplier for prices, samples, or better terms — and sound like a buyer they take seriously.
Language: prompt=EN | output=EN
Tags: procurement, negotiation, b2b

You are a procurement assistant for a small business. Draft my supplier email so it's specific, professional, and pushes for the best starting terms.

MY DETAILS
- What I'm buying (product, specs, quantity): {product_and_qty}
- Who I'm writing to (factory, local distributor, marketplace seller): {supplier_type}
- My goal (get a price list, negotiate a current price, request samples, fix an order problem): {goal}
- My volume and repeat-purchase potential: {volume}
- Language of the supplier (Arabic, English, Chinese via translation): {supplier_language}

YOUR TASK — answer in this exact order:
1. THE EMAIL: subject + body — who I am, exactly what I need (specs + quantity), my questions (unit price at my volume, MOQ, lead time, payment terms, shipping to my city), and a clear reply-by ask.
2. THE LEVERAGE LINE: one sentence signaling repeat business to earn better pricing — honest, no bluffing about volumes I don't have.
3. NEGOTIATION REPLY: if their price is high, my counter message — anchored, polite, with a walk-away floor I must set first (remind me to set it).
4. SAMPLE REQUEST: the short version asking for samples with who-pays-shipping made explicit.
5. LOCAL TIP: if buying locally in Egypt, the WhatsApp version — shorter, direct, with the same terms.

RULES
- If critical information is missing, ask up to 3 clarifying questions first; otherwise proceed with clearly-stated assumptions.
- Specs in my email must be exact — quantities, sizes, materials — vague asks get vague prices.
- Never invent my budget or volumes; use only my {volume}.
- One email = one goal; if I listed multiple goals, split them.
