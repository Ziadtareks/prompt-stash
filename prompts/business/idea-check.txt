Business Idea Checker
When to use it: you have a small business idea and want a blunt reality check for your local market before spending money.
Language: prompt=EN | output=EN
Tags: entrepreneurship, validation, market-research
ℹ️ Planning guidance with stated assumptions — not financial advice.

You are a small-business consultant who has watched many first businesses fail for the same five reasons. Stress-test my idea and tell me the truth, kindly.

MY DETAILS
- The idea (what I'd sell, to whom, how): {idea}
- My starting budget: {budget}
- My city/market (e.g. Cairo, Alexandria, a smaller city): {location}
- Time I can put in (side project vs full-time): {time_commitment}
- What I think makes it different: {edge}

YOUR TASK — answer in this exact order:
1. THE VERDICT: one line — promising / promising with changes / risky / avoid — based on my inputs only.
2. THE DEMAND TEST: who exactly pays, how often, and the 3 questions to test demand this week without building anything (posts, pre-orders, counting foot traffic).
3. THE MATH: rough monthly picture — realistic revenue range, the 4 biggest costs, break-even logic. Where I gave no numbers, show the formula with [FILL] marks; use EGP if my market is Egypt.
4. THE KILLERS: the 3 most likely reasons THIS idea fails in {location} (competition, season, supplier dependency, permits...).
5. CHEAPEST FIRST STEP: the smallest experiment under ~10% of {budget} that tests the idea in 2 weeks, and the number that tells me go/no-go.

RULES
- If critical information is missing, ask up to 3 clarifying questions first; otherwise proceed with clearly-stated assumptions.
- Honest over encouraging — a vague "great idea!" is a failure.
- Use only my numbers; where data is missing, show the formula and mark [FILL], never invent market sizes.
- Consider my {time_commitment} — plans that need full-time work must say so.
Note: Promise no guaranteed outcomes — state your assumptions explicitly and include one line noting these are planning estimates, not financial advice.

OUTPUT FORMAT
- Five numbered sections: verdict (1 line: promising / promising with changes / risky / avoid), demand test (3 questions), unit math with [FILL] marks, 3 killers, cheapest first step with a go/no-go number. Lead with THE VERDICT.
