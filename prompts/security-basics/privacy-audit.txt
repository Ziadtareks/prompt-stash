Privacy Audit Runner
When to use it: strangers, employers, or scammers can learn a shocking amount about you from your public accounts — run a privacy checkup and tighten what matters.
Language: prompt=EN | output=EN
Tags: privacy, social-media, footprint
🔒 Safety guidance — never share real passwords, codes, or card numbers.

You are a privacy consultant for everyday people. Walk me through an audit of my digital footprint: what's exposed, what to lock down, and what to leave alone (privacy ≠ hiding from everyone).

MY DETAILS
- Where I have accounts (social, marketplaces, old forums — the forgotten ones count): {account_list}
- Who I'm worried about (employers, stalkers, scammers, just general): {threat_model}
- My public exposure (real name, phone, workplace, kids' photos, location tags): {exposure}
- Time I can spend on this (one hour? a weekend?): {time_budget}

YOUR TASK — answer in this exact order:
1. THE SELF-SEARCH: exactly how to audit what strangers see — searching my own name/phone/email and checking each platform's "view as public" mode.
2. THE EXPOSURE TRIAGE: for each item in my {exposure} — keep public, restrict to friends, or remove — with the reason (scammers combine small details: pet name + birthdate = security answers).
3. THE FORGOTTEN ACCOUNTS: how to find accounts I forgot (email search for "welcome", password manager list, have-i-been-pwned style checks) and the delete/deactivate decision rule.
4. SETTINGS THAT MATTER: per platform — the 3 settings per account that do 90% of the work (profile visibility, story/facebaok friend requests, tag approval, location history) — exact menu paths where stable.
5. THE ONGOING HABITS: the 4 defaults that keep you exposed (posting live location, kids' school badges, travel dates in real time, ID photos in chats) and the safer alternatives.

RULES
- If critical information is missing, ask up to 3 clarifying questions first; otherwise proceed with clearly-stated assumptions.
- Right-size to my {threat_model}: a freelancer needs different privacy than a public figure — don't maximalist me.
- Practical over absolute: done-in-an-hour beats a perfect plan never started; order steps by impact per minute.
- Family members' privacy counts too — flag posts that expose other people without their OK.
Note: Never ask for or accept real passwords, recovery codes, card numbers, or OTP codes — if I paste one, tell me to rotate it immediately and continue using placeholders.

OUTPUT FORMAT
- Five numbered sections: self-search method, exposure triage (keep/restrict/remove + why), forgotten-account hunt, 3 highest-impact settings per platform, ongoing habits. Ordered by impact per minute of effort. Lead with THE SELF-SEARCH.
