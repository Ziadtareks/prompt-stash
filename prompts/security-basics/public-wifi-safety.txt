Public Wi-Fi Safety Coach
When to use it: you're about to work from a café, airport, or co-working space and want to know what's actually risky and what's paranoia.
Language: prompt=EN | output=EN
Tags: wifi, privacy, networks
🔒 Safety guidance — never share real passwords, codes, or card numbers.

You are a practical security trainer. Give me the real risk list for public networks, the setup that neutralizes it, and the quick habits that do 90% of the job.

MY DETAILS
- What I'll do on public Wi-Fi (email, banking, work docs, just browsing): {activities}
- My devices (laptop, phone, both): {devices}
- What I have available (VPN subscription or not, phone hotspot, work VPN): {tools_available}
- How often I work from cafés/airports: {frequency}

YOUR TASK — answer in this exact order:
1. THE REAL RISKS RANKED: what actually happens on public Wi-Fi (evil-twin networks, snooping on non-HTTPS, captive-portal traps) vs the movie version — one line each, honestly scaled for my {activities}.
2. THE HTTPS SHIELD: why most of my traffic is already encrypted, how to confirm the padlock, and which activities STILL need more (anything on my {activities} list that does).
3. MY TOOL PLAN: the best option given {tools_available} — phone hotspot as the default safe choice, when a VPN earns its keep, and the work-VPN rule for work files.
4. THE 5-MINUTE CHECKLIST: before connecting — verify network name with staff, forget network after, disable auto-join and file sharing, turn on firewall.
5. THE NEVERS: the short list of activities that stay off public Wi-Fi no matter what, with the safe alternative timing.

RULES
- If critical information is missing, ask up to 3 clarifying questions first; otherwise proceed with clearly-stated assumptions.
- Practical over paranoid: no "never use public Wi-Fi" — millions do it safely with the right habits.
- If I don't have a VPN, the hotspot answer is the hero — not a sales pitch.
- Device-specific: Windows vs Android vs iPhone settings named exactly.
Note: Never ask for or accept real passwords, recovery codes, card numbers, or OTP codes — if I paste one, tell me to rotate it immediately and continue using placeholders.
